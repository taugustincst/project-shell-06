## Full description
**A detective mystery deduction game: six closed cases from the 1930s, solved from the evidence on the page.**

**Deduction, not guessing.** You are the person they hand the impossible casebook to. No interrogation mini-games, no hidden objects: manifests, timetables, ticket stubs, logbooks and interviews, and your own reasoning.

**Six closed cases.** A night train standing at Preston. A village fête during the terrier judging. A trawler hove to in fog. A grand hotel at midnight on New Year's Eve. A circus special in the sidings at dawn. And the 9:15 again, a year on.

**Confirmed in threes.** For each person you enter who they are, where they were, and whether their word can be trusted. You may hold three entries in pencil; when all three are right they take ink together. When they are not, the book says nothing, and the case's close records every three that did not take ink.

**Airtight.** Every case is checked by a solver to have exactly one solution, reachable by deduction from the paper alone. Each person either tells the truth in every claim or lies in every claim, and you learn to tell which.

**One thread.** Behind each case stands the same man, glimpsed a little more each time, and the finale closes the book on him.

- The first case is free to play; one purchase opens the other five, the finale included.
- 55 people, 97 pieces of evidence and 165 casebook entries across the six cases.
- Three casebooks, and any case can be started again.
- No timers, no ads, no accounts.
- Offline game: plays with no connection.
- Large-text and high-contrast options; typography is the art.
